# Skin-tone stratification of the Phase 3 segmentation (pilot run + public portraits)

Date: 2026-11-11. Two parts: (1) the 2026-11-10 EasyPortrait pilot run (N = 600, same per-image rows as
`easyportrait_test_2026-11-10.md`) re-stratified by an objective skin-tone proxy; (2) a qualitative run of the full
`/api/facial-analysis/segmentation` pipeline on 35 public-domain official portraits fetched from Wikimedia Commons.
Numbers in part 1 are *agreement with EasyPortrait annotations* (dataset-level IoU, full image, denominator A unless
stated), not an accuracy figure. Aggregates in `skin_tone_2026-11-11.json`. No image, annotation or per-image row is
committed (D5); the portraits were downloaded to a scratch directory outside the repository.

## Skin-tone proxy

Skin tone is measured as the **Individual Typology Angle (ITA)** of the ground-truth skin pixels (EasyPortrait class
SKIN) or, for the portraits, of our own parsed skin: convert to CIE L\*a\*b\* (OpenCV, D65), drop L\* < 5 or > 95
(specular and deep shadow), take the per-image median L\* and b\*, ITA = atan2(L\* - 50, b\*) in degrees. Bands follow
Chardon et al.: very_light > 55, light 41-55, intermediate 28-41, tan 10-28, brown -30-10, dark <= -30.
**ITA is apparent tone under the photo's lighting and white balance, not the subject's skin type**: well-lit studio
portraits read one or two bands lighter than the person would be classified in daylight. It is still a far better
proxy than the GT-skin luma stratum used in the pilot report (Spearman luma vs ITA = 0.87, so luma mixes exposure and
tone). The pilot sample has ITA percentiles p5 -70, p25 -1, p50 29, p75 49, p95 75: all six bands are populated and
none is low-sample (n >= 83).

### Against the plan's worst-stratum gap target (<= 0.05 skin / <= 0.08 small regions, lower CI bound)

Gap = overall IoU minus the worst band's lower CI bound (point gap in brackets).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| skin | light | 0.048 (0.014) | 0.050 (0.010) | 0.05 | yes (at the limit) |

| eye | very_light / tan | **0.102** (0.024) | 0.062 (0.024) | 0.08 | **no on all images; yes without glasses** |
| brow | tan | 0.076 (0.035) | 0.076 (0.031) | 0.08 | yes |

Reading: the one miss is the eye class in the **very_light** band and it is a glasses artefact, not a tone effect:
that band holds 10 glasses images (eye IoU 0.00 each, see report 18), which widen its CI to 0.615-0.757. With the
48 glasses images removed (they are already handled as a separate signal, WP6-G) every class meets the target in every
band, and the worst band for eyes moves to tan at a gap of 0.062. Point gaps are 0.01-0.035 everywhere. The terciles
meet the target in all four classes (largest lower-CI gap 0.061, eyes, lightest third). Note that the worst band is
never the dark or brown band for any class on the point estimate; the lightest bands are slightly worse for lips and
eyes, and tan is worst for brows. Decision rule 3's "luma-stratum gap > 0.10" is not triggered on any point estimate
(largest 0.035).

### Detection failures (decision rules 1 and 4)

Both pilot failures are in the **dark** band: 2 of 83 = 2.4 % (`no_face` from the MediaPipe landmarker, Phase 2), both
1440 x 1920 images with ITA -78 and -76 (darkest 5 % of the sample) and GT skin luma 87 and 57 (underexposed). Every
other band detected 100 %. The ≤ 2 % rule-1 threshold is for faces that are fully visible, which was not checked for
these two images, and n = 2 is too small to call a rate; but the pattern (darkest, dimmest images) is the one
tone-linked finding in this report and belongs to the **detector lever** (rule 4), not the parser. Recommended check
before launch: a brightness/ITA sweep of detection on a larger dark-band sample, and the gamma-0.5 robustness result
(detection 99/100) suggests exposure rather than tone is the trigger.

## Part 2: public portraits through the full pipeline (qualitative)

35 official portraits of US public officials, all tagged Public domain on Wikimedia Commons (US federal government
works), fetched through the Commons API with a generic client string on 2026-11-11 and processed with the production
service (`face_regions.compute_face_segmentation` path, zones included). One query ("Gretchen Whitmer official
portrait") returned no public-domain hit and was skipped. ITA here is measured on our own parsed skin, so it is the
apparent tone of a well-lit studio portrait. One file (Bernie Sanders, 104th Congress) is grayscale (b\* = 0 for
every pixel, so its ITA is the degenerate 90 degrees); it is counted for status but excluded from tone statistics.

Band counts (34 colour images): dark 4, brown 2, tan 10, intermediate 11, light 5, very_light 2.

**Outcome: 35 / 35 `status: ok`, no `no_face` or `multiple_faces`, no side swaps, no pipeline errors.** Image sizes
335-1920 wide, 410-2986 high. Timings on this machine (Apple M2 Pro, unknown power state, not the reference
measurement): median total 294 ms, p95 461 ms (zones 100 / 205 ms, inference 83 / 85 ms).

Warnings: `glasses_detected` on all 9 portraits with glasses (including rimless frames at 2.3 % glasses area) and on
none without (0 false alarms, 0 misses; glasses area 2.2-6.0 %, threshold 1 %); each of those also carries
`parser_landmark_mismatch`, and 3 carry `low_overlap_zone`; eye and under-eye zones are `unavailable` or
`low_confidence` there, as designed. `hair_occludes_forehead` on 6 portraits with fringes. No other warnings.

Zone confidence versus tone (26 colour portraits without glasses): no tone trend for forehead, cheeks, chin, under-eye,
eyes, brows or lips: band means differ by at most 0.03 between the dark/brown group (ITA <= 10, n = 4) and the
intermediate-and-lighter group (n = 13), and Spearman(ITA, confidence) is between -0.19 and +0.39. The exception
worth watching is the **nose** zone, whose confidence (parser nose vs landmark nose-hull IoU) is 0.30-0.41 for every
portrait, right at its 0.30 `low_confidence` threshold; the three portraits that fall just under 0.30 and are reported
`low_confidence` are the darkest portrait (Austin, ITA -72), one tan portrait (Jeffries, ITA 16) and one glasses case
(Warnock, ITA -14); Spearman(ITA, nose confidence) +0.41, dark/brown mean 0.325 vs lighter mean 0.362. The nose masks look correct in the overlays, so this is a threshold calibrated too
close to the typical value rather than a parsing failure, but it would surface as a nose "low confidence" label more
often for darker users. n = 4 in the dark/brown group; re-check on the pilot set once a nose ground truth exists
(EasyPortrait has none).

Overlay inspection (side-by-side, label colours plus landmark zone outlines), 11 portraits viewed across all bands:
skin, nose, brows, eyes, lips and ears are placed correctly on every dark, brown, tan, intermediate, light and
very_light portrait inspected (Austin, Scott, Crockett, Jackson, Jeffries, Pressley, Lieu, Harris, Biden, Warnock,
Johnson). Non-tone findings: a hijab is parsed as `hat` (28 % of the image, Omar; status ok); large earrings and a
necklace are parsed as `earring` / `necklace` (correct); on one outdoor, back-lit portrait (Johnson, intermediate,
glasses) the parser labels the whole forehead as hair, so `forehead` is `unavailable` and `brow_r` is `low_confidence`.

## Conclusions and caveats

- On the pilot set there is **no measurable disadvantage for darker skin** in any of the four scored classes: dark and
  brown bands are at or above the overall IoU for skin, lips and eyes, and within 0.035 for brows. The lightest bands
  are marginally worse for lips and eyes. All worst-stratum gaps meet the plan's targets once the glasses stratum,
  which is a separate known failure, is excluded; with glasses included the eye class in the very_light band exceeds
  the 0.08 lower-CI gap (0.102).
- Two tone-linked items to carry forward: the 2 dark-band detection failures (underexposed, darkest 5 %), a
  detector-side check; and the nose-zone threshold sitting at the typical confidence, which would label noses
  "low confidence" slightly more often for darker faces.
- Both samples are limited: EasyPortrait is a selfie-style dataset from one source, and official portraits are evenly
  lit and professionally retouched, so neither covers low-light phone selfies of dark skin, which is the case most
  likely to fail (see the gamma and detection notes). ITA bands are apparent, not Fitzpatrick types, and no
  self-reported skin type is available for either sample.
- The ITA stratification script is not part of `backend/evaluation/` yet (it was run from a scratch directory against
  the cached pilot rows); folding it into the report generator is a small follow-up if these numbers are to be
  regenerated.
